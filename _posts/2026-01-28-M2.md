Earthbound was a very wacky game. Lot of pop-culture references, and hilarious meta jokes that the developers wove into the game. And I think it used it's very good sense of comedic timing to great effect in the game.

Playing as Ness who lives out in the suburbs of Onett, a meteorite crashes in his backyard with a strange alien emerging out of the crater with a bee warning Ness that an alien named Giygas will destroy the world... unless he and 3 other kids stop him by accumulating power at 8 sanctuaries. The plot moves on from there with several detours, but eventually they find out that Giygas is operating from the past.

Giygas is overall the villain of the game, which... he was fine I guess? It's not really like he was present throughout the game, he was more of like a calamity that was inevitable. Nothing partiuclarly interesting here.

I think Pokey was a pretty fascinating character. Victim of child abuse, jealous of Ness and him being the chosen one, and talented at everything he tried. He eventually interacts with the Mani Mani statue and ends up sucuumbing to Giygas' influence, where the worst traits of his inferiority complex comes out and he becomes more and more of a villain every time we see him. It's honestly sad and a little pathetic, especially when you find out that all he ever wanted was to be friends with Ness.

The other main party members were fine. Paula was cute and I do like to think her and Ness ended up together. She was really the heart of the team as the one who ultimately drew Ness, Jeff and Poo in to help. Jeff was a pretty interesting guy as someone who was abandoned by his parents, mostly friendless until hearing, is the only member of the party who can't use magic, but a genius in his own right and eventually shows that he was instrumental in helping them travel to the past. And Poo was also a fine character, even if he was a bit forgettable. Really, his name is the most memorable thing about him, LMAO

The main theme of this game was ultimiately how community can overcome anything, and this can be seen in the final fight against Giygas as he basically wipes out the party and is invincible. But the key to defeating him was in what was originally seemed to be useless in the prayer mechanic with Paula, as she begins to call on many of the people who the party met throughout the game to help give them strength, and the beautiful thing is, the party's actions throughout the game were able to move them deeply enough for them to hear their cries across time.

Like all Mother games, there's a scrolling healthbar, which makes for some interesting shenanigans and you can honestly get away with taking on stronger enemies than you realistically should because it takes a bit for your healthbar to fully deplete. Other than that though, nothing particularly special about this, mostly a standard turn-based JRPG.

As for the world, this was the greatest strength of the game, in my mind. Interacting with people at the mall to sell you weapons, using ATMs, weird NPCs, hilarious diologue. A variety of different locations with Onett, Twoson, Threed, Fourside and the other locations like the desert, Saturn Valley, and Poo's kingdom in the sky. It was charming, and it leaned heavily into this fact.

I think Earthbound wasn't a perfect game. It was repetitive at times, sometimes felt a bit grindy if you felt like you were getting too weak before a boss fight. Inventory management was also a slog at times. But it was a game clearly made with a lot of love and care, and I think it was a game that had a great message at the end of the day. And getting to walk backwards through the world and see how your actions affected the NPCs before closing off the credits at Ness' House was a brilliant way to end the game. It was unique and a fun game! Definitely give it a fair shake.
