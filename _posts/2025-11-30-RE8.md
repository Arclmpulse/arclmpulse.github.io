RE8 picks up a couple of years following the fallout of what happened in the Baker Estate after Ethan successfully extracted his wife, as Chris Redfield chooses to relocate him and his pregnant wife to a remote cottage in Romania. Of course, the location that he picked just so happened to be near a very well documented and long history of a cult nearby...

Plot was overall pretty good. I find the plothole of why BSAA moved the Winters to Romania in the first place to be very illogical but lets ignore that fact, shall we? The game kicks off with Mia and their daughter Rose getting kidnapped by local countryside cult leader, Miranda. Ethan then goes on a crazy adventure through the local village, into a nearby castle, through a haunted manor, traversing through a swamp and lastly a nearby factory to assemble all the keys and stop Miranda from sacrificing Rose to revive her daughter in a gated off shrine. All the while, learning more and more about the origins of the mold he first encountered in Dulvey, Louisiana from RE7.

Ethan was the same as RE7 Ethan more or less - a pretty normal/average family man who found himself in an even more crazy and fucked up scenario than RE7. We actually get the explanation of some cutscenes from RE7 here - Ethan and Mia are fully revealed to be made of mold, and the human Ethan actually died when Jack Baker kicked his skull in in the prologue of the game, fully explaining Ethan's superhuman healing abilities. Mia presumably died at some point when she was imprisoned in the estate, though the serum you gave her during RE7 mostly freed the mold's influence from her. I think he is a fairly vanilla personality compared to the big 4 main protagonists in the series, but RE8 was a game that showed how far a parent's love could go for their child, and Ethan embodied that beatuifully when he sacrificed himself for Rose and Chris at the end.

Miranda as the main villain of RE8 and ultimately the duology of RE7/RE8 showed off the flipside of this: how horrible the loss of a child can be to a parent. We learn that bioweapon and main villain from RE7 Eveline was an experiment created by Miranda when she discovered a special type of mold with healing properties in the Romanian countryside following the loss of her own child (Eva) from the spanish flu... in 1919. Of course, she was a scientist as well, and manipulated what was once a neutral and maybe even a healing compound into a horribly mutating, all-consuming zombifying fungus with the potential side effect of supernatural powers and healing. Rose ended up being the perfect vessel of her plan to resurrect her daughter - a child purely made of mold with its consciousness intact so that she could imprint her daughter's consciousness into Rose.

And we had the other secondary villains, each of them representing some sort of genre of horror after the mold mutated them. Lady Dimetrescu is the famous one. Funnily enough I thought she was kind of a boring villain, though I do think the castle is the most well designed part of the game with the puzzles - it was clear the game devs spent the most time there and it was a nice call back to the thriller type of horror RE2 introduced and RE7 built on.

Donna Beneviento and her house was by far my favourite part of the game - I'm a sucker for psychological horrors, and exploring the creepy, unsettling house filled with dolls, puppets, hallucinogens and a creepy giant baby fetus chasing you around the basement Alien Isolation style is something I will never forget. I found her story to be a bit sad.

Salvatore Moreau wasn't anything special, but I did feel sorry for the guy as someone who just wanted someone's approval ending up mutating into something... grotesque. Running around in his swamp was fun, and the boss fight was kinda like Jaws, haha.

And lastly the Karl Heisenberg's factory leaned more into the Frankenstein type of horror of splicing mold, machine, and humans together. While his segment was the most fighting filled, I think Heisenberg was an interesting character as someone who wanted to usurp Miranda and take over the area himself.

Gameplay was super fun. RE8 built off of the movement and gameplay RE2R/RE3R and RE7 introduced, and I think it was done pretty well. Puzzles were done very well - I think some of the best in the series are done here with Beneviento and Lady D's castle. They also brought in a travelling merchant like RE4 had, which was really nice.

Oh boy, some of the flaws of the game. Well, the plot is kinda dumb honestly, but I won't go into that since I touched on it already. I do think some areas got far more focus and time than others. Moreau and Beneviento clearly were given less time for development when you can run through their sections super quickly, while the Castle and the Factory will likely take hours. I'm not a big fan of the action shift here, especially since RE7 did such a good job of bringing the core gameplay elements back to classical horror. And honestly, you don't learn enough about Miranda unless you seek out the extra documents and notes, which does make her a weaker villain in my mind. And of course, Chris gets brought in for like 15-20 mins of screentime again, lol. Might as well just cut him from the game or make him the main protagonist Capcom, don't do this weird inbetween thing!!!

OST was good though. Village of Shadows is iconic, so is Yearning for Dark Shadows.

All in all, it was a good game. Some design choices that were questionable, but I do think the "themepark of horror" angle they went for in the village as the connecting hub between them was done very well. Some flaws I can't overlook, but it closed off Ethan Winters' arc nicely.
