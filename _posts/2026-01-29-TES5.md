This might be controversial. I don't think Skyrim is an amazing game. Good, yes. Great even. But absolutely not the pinnacle of the series.

Plot is probably the most famous one in the series. Starting off as a prisoner about to get publicly executed, you get bailed out by a giant, apocalyptic dragon wreaking havoc in the town who was sent forward through time and landed exactly on this era. The plot opens from here as you make your way to one of the major cities in the country and it's eventually revealed that you're a Dragonborn (even though you're not related to the Septim dynasty. Unless..?), and the only one who can properly stop Alduin from "eating the world" as his title suggests.

Honestly, my thoughts on the plot were that it was a lot more simple/straightforward than the other two. Which was fine, but I do feel a bit sad that it felt like a small step down and felt a bit more generic.

Alduin was a fine villain. We barely get much personality or lore about him through the main plot but he 100% is an absolute force as he was described to be from the lore. His angle to defy his role as the one to create a new world by destroying the current one and instead follow through with his desire to try to rule over the current one by subjugating his fellow dragons and normal humans as a whole was kinda an interesting subversion of the "apocalyptic threat" that many games like to go with. And it is kinda ironic that because Alduin is defying his fate, it allows him to be vulnerable and sent away into the future once more like the older heroes did. Non super present in the game, but a fun boss fight at the end for sure.

Combat... I have mixed reviews on this. Melee weapons feel great, much better than from TES3/4. Archery is also much improved and feels really fantastic too. Stealth Archer anyone? Magic is my criticism. The magical system took way too many steps back and feels very, very dumbed down with no ability to create your own spells in this game. And what's worse is that realistically, only destruction magic is viable in this game.

Most of the levelling systems are the same as Oblivion, using skills improves them, which is fine. They did away with the sleep to level up system and locking enemy encounters to your level, which was much needed.

The world is incredible. It probably was the game that blew up the open world genre and created an influx of these types of games for the next decade. And for good reason, Skyrim is so diverse climate-wise that going towards the mountains near Markarth, snowy tundra lands near Windhelm, lush forests down below Whiterun and really pretty fauna near Riften, there was a bit of everything. This was probably the last game that had really good Bethesda cities, would've been even better if there were no loading zones but it was still great seeing how every NPC has a schedule.

Sidequests were ok for the most part, Blood on the Ice was my personal favourite one. But overall a downgrade from TES4's side quests. And, oh dear, the factions in this game are HORRIBLE. Companions were a very watered down version of TES4's Fighter's Guild with a werewolf subplot. Thieves Guild was had some weird Nocturnal cult subplot that I did not enjoy, and honestly you could argue these guys were the Thug's Guild with how little stealing you need to do. You can literally become Archmage of College of Winterhold without casting a single spell, which is hilarious but also really sad. And while I think Dark Brotherhood in this game was the strongest faction narratively, it paled in comparison to TES4's DB, which is one of the best pieces of side content in a game, ever.

Moving onto DLCs, I think Dawnguard was a fun campaign with vampires and Lord Harkon. It wasn't anything special, but it was a good story with a satisfying ending. Dragonborn made me emotional when we revisited Raven Rock and Solstheim. Miraak was the villain here and honestly the Dragonborn campaign felt like the epilogue of the main story, getting the Last Dragonborn (you) to square off against the First Dragonborn (Miraak) over becoming tentacle daedra monster Hermaeus Mora's official protege was the bizarre and crazy plot that we know and love Elder Scrolls for.

A lot of beautiful ambience themes in this game, but Solstheim 11 is my favourite.

I don't think Skyrim was a bad game. It was a great game, which had amazing elements to it, but also took a step back in many departments. It probably becomes a 10/10 game once you factor in mods, but the base game alone I think was a game that was special, but not incredible.
