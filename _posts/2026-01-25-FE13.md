I know people say this a lot, but it cannot be stated enough. If this game flopped, Intelligent Systems would've pulled the plug on the series so they went all out with the characters and references in the game to other games. So this game unironically saved the franchise from being axed by Nintendo, which is why so many fans of the franchise will always be fond of this game. Also, fun fact! This game is the same continent as FE1/3/11/12 if you look closely at the geography! Just set thousands of years in the future.

Plot was messy but one of the better ones in the series once you can make sense of it. Starting with an interesting sequence in the prologue to get your feet wet, Robin/My Unit wakes up in a field where Chrom and Lissa wake them up where they have amnesia, realize they're a gifted tactician, and offers them a chance to help fight back against a warring neighbouring country, which makes up the first act of the game. Then the second act of the game focuses on the second continent of Valm as they launch a conquest on Ylisse. Before bringing it all back together and the real threat being Grima, an apocalyptic dragon who wants to wipe out humanity. The plot twist here is that from the opening sequence and the amnesia on Robin, we think Robin is a time traveller, sent back to help Chrom and the rest of the world avoid doomsday. But once Grima/Evil Robin gets revealed in Act 3, its the other way around - Robin was meant to become evil eventually in his current timeline. But Lucina and the children of the main cast went back in time to stop this future from happening. But Evil Robin/Grima followed them back as well!

Yikes, that was a mouthful. Grima as the main villain of the game was done fairly well. I kinda liked when there were no apocalyptic dragons as the main villain of the game, but I think the time travel plot added to the uniqueness of Grima as a villain in the series. It was interesting to see Evil Robin/Grima as that showed off what would've happened to Robin had Validar properly raised them up to be Grima's avatar sooner and if Robin hadn't been on the run for their entire life.

Robin is a decent lead. It's kinda hard to evaluate him/her as a character when Chrom is treated like the main character of the game with him being the one that must be included in every story level, and the fact that Robin is sort of a blank slate character. Inoffensive and fine is probably the best word for them. Lot of funny supports with Robin, and I wouldn't really say they grew as a character over the game, but it was a plot that ultimately centered around Robin and their final choice to kill Grima without knowing what would happen to Robin themselves.

Chrom is kind of the main character of the game and is the first Lord you get to play with. While I think he had a strong emotional arc with his older sister getting murdered in the Plegia/Ylisse war in Act 1 and him overcoming that and becoming a true King, his arc really just falls off from there as we don't really see the same highs in his character.

And Lucina pretending to be Marth early on and fully linking up in Act 2 with the team as the third main character, second Lord and Chrom's child from the future was interesting to say the least. I will say her conflict with Robin as she slowly realizes the friend who betrayed her father and triggered the apocalypse was Robin all along was probably the highlight of the game, and doubly so if she's Robin's S-support. But like Chrom, her plot relevance fades past Act 2.

Gameplay was... interesting. This game added the pair-up mechanic which was kinda unique. It let anyone be "rescued" or do the rescuing. Further, adjacent units and paired units could help out in combat. This sort of broke combat and forced them to make enemies way harder and swarm in waves on the map to compensate the fact that you'd just one rotation every single enemy with a good formation. And of course, the much overhauled support system that let you have infinite supports with all characters (but only one romance) and it tying into playing eugenics with the time travelled children that become playable post Act 1 was hilarious. I enjoyed shipping off my characters, and it was fun to see how the personalities of the children turned out, haha. It was at the expense of having more deeper/meaningful interactions between the characters though, felt like most of the supports were shallow.

I will say, if you play as female Robin, I think Chrom x F!Robin is canon with Lucina as their daughter. And if you play as M!Robin (personally I think Male Robin is canon), then M!Robin x Lucina is canon with Sumia as Chrom's wife. Content-wise it just makes the most sense to me.

Maps were sort of frustrating. Felt like a majority of the maps were just open fields with some trees or buildings sprinkled in. I think the indoor maps were done well, but there aren't really many standout maps which is sad. I think 13-10 (Renewal) was a highlight after Emmeryn falls and Chrom and his Shepherds are forced to retreat out of the valley. But other than that... not really much else.

I will say, while it's easy to kill single troops in the game, higher difficulties can get hard very quickly if you're not prepared for how fast enemies will swarm you on their turn. I think FE13 got that right.

OST slapped, as per usual with FE games. Don't Speak Her Name (13-10 theme), and Id/Purpose (13-Endgame) are the two most iconic themes in the game, and for good reason. The former for the heavy emotions, and the latter for what may have been the final fight of the entire franchise, it felt right as a hopeful, triumphant anthem to send off the series.

Robin, Chrom and Lucina saved the franchise and created a second wind in the series, and I will always praise the devs for making a game that had mainstream appeal. But I can't really say this was the best game FE had to offer. And it sort of was the game that made people more interested in the series as more of a dating sim type series, which was... interesting, I guess. Recommend for sure! But I do think it gets overrated a bit.
