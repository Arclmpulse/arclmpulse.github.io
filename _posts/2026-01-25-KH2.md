This game was peak Nomura. Edgy character designs, vague shadowy group that orchestrates the plot, obsession with destiny and interconnected characters. That red string of fate that binds two dudes! Shame he just keeps reusing these tropes like a one trick pony in his more recent games though, maybe Kingdom Hearts taking off like it did was a mistake for the company...?

Plot is overall probably the best of the main KH games. The intro sequence with Roxas in Twilight Town is one of the greatest prologues in a game I've ever played, and it setup the rest of the game perfectly as you begin to wonder who Roxas really is before the game puts you back to playing as Sora. And the plot unfolding from there as Organization XIII took centre stage with the concept of nobodies being introduced (the husk of the body that gets left behind when a heartless is created) and the heartless returning from the previous game as Org XIII's motivations to open up Kingdom Hearts to try and become human was executed very well. I think KH1 worked very good as a standalone game but this game retconned Ansem a bit and made Xehanort/Xenmas the actual villain of the series, which I think was fine with how they developed Org XIII.

Sora is more or less the same guy as KH1 Sora, just went through puberty a bit. I think he was fine. Pretty goofy guy with a good heart trying to save his old friends and return back to his old world, he worked well in the plot.

Roxas is the bigger standout as a character who got introduced - while it's obvious he was connected to Sora somehow from the intro, it doesn't really hit you until you visit Twilight Town again later in the game that, Roxas was Sora's nobody from the heartless that was created in KH1 who had a burning desire to become his own individual person. However, the struggle began when it got revealed that Roxas and his human-like qualities couldn't exist as long as his real self was conscious, and the only reason why he was active with his own life in Twilight Town was because Sora had gotten captured, lost his memories and needed to recuperate in a pod. It made his final line from the prologue _"My summer vacation is over"_ far more impactful as he's forced to reunite with Sora and cause him to awaken, as well as making his boss fight in the last arc of the game far more emotional because of the stakes of what he was trying to fight for inside Sora's mind, and also made him finally accepting his fate to reunite with Sora more heartbreaking. Not sure how to feel when he gets to come back later in the series though...

As for villains, Xenmas was a very good villain in this one as the other side of Xehanort, with KH1 Ansem being the first half of Xehanort. Gathering all of the powerful nobodies like a cult leader in Org XIII and selling them on a lie that they can become human too was diabolical work. His goal was of course being to open Kingdom Hearts and harness its power like Ansem, and he did come dangerously close too.

I have to call out the flaws in the plot now. What's the point of all the disney worlds being present if nothing to advance the overall plot happened in almost all of them? Literally only needed Twilight Town, Hollow Bastion, Mickey's Castle, Tron's world and The World that Never Was, felt like the rest of the worlds were filler. Not to mention how Maleficient and Pete were highlighted so much in the first half of the game but completely disappeared by the end. Further, it felt like we barely explored each Disney world because we were forced to visit them twice, the visits felt way too short in comparison to KH1. And don't get me started on how half of Organization XIII were filler villains...

As for the worlds themselves, the ones we went to were pretty fun. Agrabah, Atlantica (as a sidequest world thankfully), Halloween Town (Christmas Edition!), Herc's Colosseum, and 100 Acre Wood/Winnie the Pooh all return in this game, and Olympus even got fleshed out a bit more. As for the new ones, Land of Dragons/Mulan, Belle's Castle, Port Royal/Pirates of the Caribbean and Pride Lands/Lion King are the new adds, and all of them were great. Port Royal in particular, the graphics were really impressive for a 2005 game. And I have to shoutout Mickey's Castle with the Timeless River, getting to play in a 1930's cartoon world was fantastic.

Combat in this game is legendary, and for good reason. I don't think I've ever had so much fun in a JRPG before, it might be up there as a contender for one of the best ever. Real time combat, Final Fantasy magic/summons, Driver modes adding 4 different styles of combat you could lean into, ridiculous combos you can pull off, movement, jumping, reaction commands, this game gives you so much choice and lets you go wild.

The OST is iconic, the best version of Fragments of Sorrow is in this game, I think my personal favourtite version of Dearly Beloved is in this one too. And of course, some new tracks like 13th Struggle or the Twilight Town themes are great. Especially the latter, theres a weird wistfulness in the theme that feels a little emotional when you first hear it, and once you learn the point of Twilight Town, you really understand why.

I love KH2, it was one of the first video games I ever played. It posed an interesting question on humanity, and if "emotionless bodies" can truly be human. But as much as it's one of my favourite games of all time, there are a lot of flaws with it too. Of course I recommend it, and I think its the best game of the series. But I can't rate it as highly as some others.
