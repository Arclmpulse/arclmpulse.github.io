I don't know how to rate this game, honestly. It's such... a weird game. Classic Kojima, I guess.

The plot of the game is confusing, but I would say it is a strength of the game. Playing as Sam Porter Bridges, the world underwent an apocalyptic event not too long ago in the past with an extinction entity triggering a merge between the afterlife with the current world. Which in turn created a horrible mess in current day America with strange demons and ghost-like figures roaming the world. Sam was the adopted child of the previous President of the USA and her daughter, Amelie recruits Sam to try and reconnect the remains of cities left in America to a network by delivering packages and trying to get them to connect to a network for them to unite with. And Sam does this... before its revealed that Amelie is an exinction entity who wanted humanity to come together one last time before wiping it out.

Death Stranding focuses a lot on the bond between parent and child. President Bridget putting all of her faith in Sam to try and reconnect America once more. The concept of using Bridge Babies (experimental babies born from braindead mothers who can detect demons and ghosts from the afterlife as they live on the verge of life and death). Lockne and Malingen's child and how Kojima showed off post-partum, and lastly Sam finally laying his BB to rest at the end of the game, it was the central theme of the game and I think they executed it well.

Sam as well was a really fascinating character. He was a bit dry as a personality, but I think it made sense given the world. The slow reveal that the ghost/hallucination he keeps experiencing throughout the game of a soldier who keeps pursuing Sam and trying to take his BB away from him before it's eventually revealed that Sam was a BB at one point was brilliant. And Sam eventually learning that he was the child of the soldier who is mixing Sam's BB up with Sam himself was a brilliant scene as Cliff is finally able to move on. Overall a good vehicle for the game, maybe a little forgettable as a character though.

I guess Amelie is the main villain of the game? It's hard to say that because of course she never wanted to take out humanity, but it is difficult to fight one's own nature/existence, and that was the point of her character. While there is a final fight in the game (not with Amelie), the last sequence of the game being Sam convincing Amelie on her beach (purgatory) to delay her triggering the extinction event further and to give America a chance to try and fix the world, and of course herself was fitting, and I do suppose it left the door open for a sequel.

Gameplay is just... bizarre. Yes, you play as an UPS deliveryman. That is the core gameplay loop, to try and deliver packages on time and create links between settlements. Fighting really isn't the main focus of the game, but you do have tools to take out enemies should you so choose to do so. Early on in the game though, you're going to feel helpless and running away is often the best strategy. Which will probably feel really bad but its realistic sadly, and Kojima loves that. Anything you build or find in this game can be accelerated by other people playing the game too in order to lean into the "everything is connected" angle, which is... kinda weird. I'm not sure if I like it or not but I guess it doesn't hurt your progress, just helps it.

Map was fun to explore though. And building roads for your little dune buggy/truck/car when you eventually unlock better vehicles eventually made the delivery missions pretty fun. But that's far, far later in the game.

I loved a lot of the indie songs they brought in, but BB's theme is probably the most iconic one which was an original song in the OST.

Thematically, this game was incredible. In practice it's frustrating and slow, especially at the start of the game. Unbearably so, at times. Kojima's classic of long, winded cutscenes can be seen in full effect here.

I think Death Stranding is a masterpiece, and as someone with no child I'm sure a parent playing this game would get far more emotional than I would. But it is also one that is hard for me to recommend to other people because of how strange of a game it is, which is a very big turnoff honestly. I'd give it a shot, but I don't blame anyone who can't finish the game.
